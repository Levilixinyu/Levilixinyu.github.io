03 / Research

Current projects

NMES · coherence

EMG–EMG coherence & electrical stimulation

How does NMES reshape the shared neural drive between shoulder muscles in people with rotator cuff disease?

Read more

NMES is a well-established rehabilitation tool known for enhancing muscle activation and promoting motor recovery. It can induce plastic changes in motor pathways, particularly within the motor cortex and corticospinal tract. By stimulating sensory pathways, NMES provides proprioceptive feedback that influences motor control programming in the central nervous system. EMG–EMG coherence assesses the frequency-domain similarity of neural inputs to functionally related muscles, giving insight into shared neural drive between motor neuron pools. Investigating these effects across conditions is critical for tailoring NMES-based rehabilitation protocols to the capabilities and recovery goals of individuals with neurological disorders.

decomposition · firing

Motor unit analysis

Does NMES alter motor unit firing rates and recruitment thresholds during slow ramp contractions?

Read more

A motor unit is the functional unit of skeletal muscle: a single motor neuron and all the muscle fibers it controls. Efficient firing rate and recruitment threshold are essential for restoring functional movement. In healthy individuals, fast-twitch motor units are recruited at higher force levels following Henneman's size principle, while in people with neuromuscular impairments these units may show delayed or inefficient activation. This study uses a series of motor unit decomposition methods to determine whether NMES alters motor unit firing patterns during slow ramp contractions.

AI4Health · LLM evaluation

Human evaluation of LLM-generated gait reports

Can language models describe stroke gait and EMG data completely, without claiming more than the data support?

Read more

A paired evaluation of three LLM configurations across 105 reports, built from public gait and EMG data of 25 stroke survivors and 10 healthy controls. I authored the system instructions and, with domain experts, co-developed case-specific evaluation criteria and a nine-category error taxonomy that separates required-content coverage from unsupported or incorrect claims. Three independent human raters completed 315 blinded report reviews. Paired bootstrap comparisons (20,000 resamples), inter-rater agreement, and sensitivity analyses showed that high content coverage can coexist with unsupported claims.

UT Austin iSchool · 2025

Clinical temporal reasoning in LLMs

Teaching an LLM to turn time-dependent clinical questions into correct SQL over MIMIC-III/IV.

Read more

With Prof. Ying Ding. A temporal-aware Text-to-SQL framework that pairs a 17-rule temporal parser with structured GPT-4.1 prompts to interpret relative, absolute, and event-based time expressions. Explicit time templates and few-shot examples raised SQL exact-match accuracy from 81.8% to 90.9% over naive prompting; execution-based evaluation on EHRSQL test cases pointed to absolute-year expressions as the main remaining error source.

LLM post-training for medical symptom triage

Fine-tuning a biomedical LLM to read symptom narratives and route patients to the right department.

Read more

With Prof. Louis Gutierrez. Fine-tuned BioMistral-7B with LoRA on 7,475 symptom narratives, then applied direct preference optimization (DPO) with clinician-preference data. Post-training improved BLEU 3.7× and reached 87.0% exact match for disease and department prediction.
